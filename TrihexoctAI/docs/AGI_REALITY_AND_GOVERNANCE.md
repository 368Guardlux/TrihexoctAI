# TRIHEXΩ v2.0.0-alpha.5 — Intelligence Architecture and AGI Reality

## What the system actually is

TRIHEXΩ contains a bounded, staged multi-agent language-model review council. It has sixteen role-specialized calls per non-empty candidate batch: twelve analysts, three critics, and one synthesis guardian. The calls use one explicitly configured LLM-compatible provider endpoint per run in this release.

Role prompts provide task specialization. They do not create sixteen independently trained models, independent minds, or genuine artificial general intelligence. The agents can share the same underlying model weaknesses, provider outages, training-data gaps, and blind spots. Agreement is not proof.

## What the council can do

- Analyze a small structured bundle of candidate indicators and evidence.
- Review evidence provenance, rule scope, source lineage, compatibility, privacy, and performance implications.
- Challenge prior findings and identify contradictory or missing evidence.
- Produce structured recommendations and unresolved blockers.

## What it cannot do

- Browse the open internet or independently discover live threats.
- Execute shell commands, download arbitrary content, or operate a browser.
- Read secrets, access repository APIs, modify source code, or change workflows.
- Change model weights, source trust, release thresholds, policies, or permissions.
- Insert or remove production filter rules or publish a release.

## Supervised learning

The feedback ledger stores explicit human outcomes with hash-chain integrity. Aggregate diagnostics can improve candidate review order and identify categories requiring additional evidence. It is not model training, and the labels are not ground truth. Provisional approvals and unresolved cases are not treated as confirmed outcomes.

The evolution planner turns operational reports into a bounded engineering backlog and tracks recurrence. It does not implement its recommendations or auto-resolve missing tasks.

## Governance principle

AI investigates and recommends. Deterministic validators enforce policy and compatibility requirements. Human maintainers decide whether a change is appropriate. Production publication remains a separate least-privilege process.
